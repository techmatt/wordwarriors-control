# Word Warriors — design

Learn-to-read game for Julia (4; knows letter names, not letter sounds; comfortable with an Xbox 360 pad and Stardew-style walking; hunt-and-peck on a keyboard). Inspired by Reader Rabbit: minigames earn a spectacle. Here the spectacle is a mock battle between characters she likes.

## Episode loop (~10–15 min)

1. She picks a hero (can be Julia) and at most one good-guy ally.
2. Alarm: the ally or Dada Matt / Dada Jason says "Bad news, Julia! Evil <villain> is coming! We need to get ready!"
3. Prep minigames (rounds of 3–5 items). Each visibly makes something the battle then uses.
4. Battle part 1: the heroes lose to the villain's over-the-top ultimate and retreat. Scripted; never framed as her failure.
5. Counter-item minigame, themed to the move that beat them.
6. Battle part 2: the counter beats the ultimate; the heroes win.

No fail states: wrong answers get a gentle retry, no penalty. Outfits remain usable as a secondary reward.

## Reading and speech

- Prep minigames: everything is voiced except the word she must read. That word is spoken only after she answers, as confirmation. Letter SOUNDS (not names) are voiced as teaching. A hint may sound out letters one by one but never blends the word.
- Battle choices: mid-battle, two or three move words (e.g. FIRE / ICE / ZAP) appear. The partner says which to pick and she finds the written word. A right pick fires a supercharged version; a wrong pick fires that move, plainer. Practice only; it changes nothing else.
- Every spoken line is data with an id. edge-tts placeholders now, Adobe TTS later.
- Progress is tracked per letter sound and per word; minigames lean toward her shaky items. A simple parent screen shows it.

## Movement and minigames

Stardew-style walking. Her character is the cursor: answers are places in the room she walks to and presses A on. The character sprite gets only light bob and squash/scale motion. Starter set:
- sound → letter: three letter stones, the voice says a sound;
- first sound: a picture, pick its starting letter;
- blend path: letter tiles say their sounds as she steps across them, left to right, ending at picture doors;
- word → picture: the word on a banner, pictures on pedestals;
- spell the spell: type a short word on the keyboard to forge the counter-item.

## Battles

Magicka-style free simulation, not a scripted director. Every fighter moves in their own style and uses abilities on their own cooldowns, each with a taunt: summons on both sides, spells (e.g. polymorph), warriors twirling through the crowd, giants that barely move, dodgers with afterimages. Colorful, overdone particles; teamwork moves. Health bars are cosmetic and steered, so the outcome is fixed: part 1 ends with the villain's ultimate, part 2 with the counter and the heroes' win. Characters, abilities, ultimates, counters and vocab are data.

Characters (each has good and evil versions): Julia, Seraphina (a girl who keeps dragons), Dada Matt, Dada Jason, Jasmine, Elsa, Anna, Ozymandias, Jörmungandr, Smaug, Scar (a robot), Moriarty, Bavmorda; more welcome.

## Art

AI art generated by Matt in ChatGPT's image model from prompts the planner writes: one shared style block plus a prompt per asset. Simple sprites, no frame animation; a good and an evil image per character; arena backgrounds; props, counter items and picture-word cards. Studio-owned characters are written as distinct archetype proxies, not lookalikes.

Browser game; Phaser 3 + TypeScript + Vite by default (Seraphina's Secret precedent; audit pending). Code may be reused from `C:\Code\seraphinas\seraphinas-secret`; its character assets are not expected to be useful.
